#### Description

`facebook/bart-large-cnn` is the BART-large checkpoint (Lewis et al., arXiv:1910.13461) fine-tuned on the CNN/DailyMail news-summarization dataset, published by Facebook AI on the Hugging Face Hub (the model card there was written by the Hugging Face team, as it says) and pinned here to revision `37f520fa929c961707657b28798b30c003dd100b`. BART is a denoising sequence-to-sequence Transformer: a 12-layer bidirectional encoder and a 12-layer autoregressive decoder with `d_model` 1024, 16 attention heads, a 50 264-entry byte-level BPE vocabulary and 1024 positions (snapshot `config.json`), 406 290 432 float32 parameters in the pinned `model.safetensors` (counted from the SafeTensors header). Pre-training corrupts text with a noising function and learns to reconstruct it; the CNN/DailyMail fine-tune then trains the decoder to emit a highlight-style summary of a news article. At inference this package encodes one document once and runs deterministic beam search on the decoder (`num_beams` 4, `length_penalty` 2.0, `no_repeat_ngram_size` 3, `early_stopping` true, and the length bounds of the snapshot's `generation_config_for_summarization.json`) to produce one summary. No adaptation happens in this repository — no training, fine-tuning or in-context conditioning; the pinned checkpoint is used as published. What this repository adds is packaging: the `BARTSummarizationPipeline` class in `src/bart_summarization_pipeline/pipeline.py`, digest verification of the local snapshot (`verify_snapshot`, `stage_missing_files`), input and generation-setting validation against named ceilings, and a fixed output contract that reports token counts and whether the summary was cut by the length ceiling.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

###### Primary Intended Uses

Abstractive summarization of one English document per call: input one string of up to `MAX_INPUT_TOKENS = 1024` BPE tokens (about three to four pages of prose); output one summary generated by beam search, together with `generated_tokens`, `input_tokens`, `truncated` (true when the decoder hit `max_new_tokens` before emitting end-of-sequence) and the generation settings used. The upstream README's own example is a multi-paragraph news article reduced to three sentences, and that is the shape of task intended: condensing news reports, press releases, meeting notes or similar expository prose into a short highlight for a human reader who can still open the source. In a larger system the pipeline is a first-draft summarizer whose output a person reviews before it is relied on; it is not a fact-extraction service and not a substitute for reading the document where the details matter.

###### Primary Intended Users

Machine-learning engineers, NLP researchers and application developers integrating an off-the-shelf news summarizer into research prototypes, internal enterprise tooling or the DIMER model workbench. The pipeline assumes its users understand that an abstractive summary can state things the source does not (hallucination) and can omit what matters most, that the model was fine-tuned on news articles paired with bullet-point highlights and will write in that register whatever the input, that the checkpoint is English-only and 406 M parameters (~1.6 GB float32, roughly 7 s to load and 4 s per 230-token document on the CPU used here), and that the pinned `min_new_tokens` default (55) forces summaries of at least that many tokens even for a short input, which the caller may lower. It is not designed for hobbyist "point and trust" use.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** not classification, question answering or translation — the `bart-mnli-zero-shot-classification-pipeline` sibling covers zero-shot classification; not extractive summarization with guaranteed source spans (the output is free text and may paraphrase or invent); not multi-document or long-document summarization — one document per call and no chunking, so a document over 1024 tokens is rejected rather than split; not headline generation or summaries shorter than the caller's `min_new_tokens`; not sampling-based or diverse generation (`do_sample` is fixed to `False`).
2. **Input boundary:** `str` only (`TypeError` otherwise); empty or whitespace-only text rejected; more than `MAX_TEXT_CHARS = 40000` characters rejected before tokenisation and more than `MAX_INPUT_TOKENS = 1024` BPE tokens (including `<s>`/`</s>`) rejected after it — inputs are never silently truncated; `max_new_tokens` outside 1–`MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 512`, `min_new_tokens` above `max_new_tokens`, `num_beams` outside 1–`MAX_NUM_BEAMS = 8`, `length_penalty` outside `LENGTH_PENALTY_RANGE = (-5.0, 5.0)` and `no_repeat_ngram_size` outside 0–`MAX_NO_REPEAT_NGRAM_SIZE = 10` are rejected. Non-English text, code, tables, dialogue transcripts and text far from the news register degrade without any warning from the pipeline.
3. **Decision boundary:** not for autonomous or high-impact decisions — summarising medical records, legal filings, financial disclosures or safety reports for someone who will act without reading the source — without a human comparing the summary against the document and a locally measured faithfulness rate; not for generating text presented as a person's own words.

#### Factors

###### Groups

The pipeline is human-centric in the sense that its inputs are news-style text about people and its output restates what those texts say about them, choosing which names, attributes and events to keep. The upstream README states the fine-tuning data as CNN/DailyMail and carries no bias or group-level statement of its own; CNN/DailyMail consists of English news articles from two outlets with their editor-written highlights, and neither the upstream card nor this repository reports any group-level analysis of whose stories are covered, how they are framed, or which subjects a summary tends to drop. This repository ran no fairness evaluation. The obligation therefore transfers to the operator: before any deployment whose summaries concern people, compare summaries of paired documents that differ only in a group term (names, pronouns, nationalities) for what is kept, dropped or altered, evaluate faithfulness stratified by the groups relevant to the application on the operator's own documents, and treat a material gap as a blocker.

###### Instrumentation

The training data was captured by no physical sensor: it is text. The upstream README discloses the fine-tuning set by name only — CNN/DailyMail, "a large collection of text-summary pairs" — and the BART paper discloses the pre-training data as the same corpus RoBERTa used (books, news, web text and stories). The instrument characteristics that reach the model are therefore the news outlets' editorial and publishing pipelines and the scraping and highlight-extraction procedure that built CNN/DailyMail, none of which the pinned README describes. At inference the only instrument is the byte-level BPE tokenizer shipped in the snapshot (`vocab.json`, `merges.txt`, `tokenizer.json`); unusual Unicode, OCR errors, code, URLs and tables fragment into many sub-word pieces, and the pipeline reports the input token count (`input_tokens`) but does not detect encoding drift, language change or OCR noise.

###### Environment

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `tokenizers==0.22.2`, `numpy==2.5.3` (exact pins in `pyproject.toml`), float32. This is a 406 M-parameter model whose `model.safetensors` is 1 625 222 120 bytes, so it needs roughly 1.7 GB of free RAM for the weights alone, and beam search with 4 beams over up to 141 decoder steps is the slow half of a call. `from_pretrained(device=None)` picks `cuda:0` when available, else CPU; this repository's smoke ran on CPU only (Windows venv, `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1`, `device="cpu"`): loading and digest-verifying the 1.63 GB snapshot took 6.72 s and one `summarize` call on a 232-token synthetic passage with the pinned defaults took 4.28 s. The CUDA path is untested in this repository. Data environment: inputs are assumed to be English news-style expository prose of a few hundred to a thousand tokens, resembling the CNN/DailyMail articles the decoder was fine-tuned on; behaviour on other languages, dialogue, scientific or legal text, lists and tables, or very short inputs (where the 55-token minimum forces padding-out) is not measured here and is expected to degrade.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

The pipeline reports no performance measure. `summarize` emits free text plus counts and flags (`generated_tokens`, `input_tokens`, `truncated`, `stopped_by`); none of them is a quality score, and the repository ships no ROUGE, BERTScore or faithfulness helper, so no metric is computed. To evaluate the summaries a caller must supply one or more reference summaries per document from the deployment domain and compute ROUGE-1/2/L (or a learned metric) with their own scorer over enough documents to state a dispersion, and must separately check faithfulness against the source because ROUGE rewards overlap, not truth. The public `evaluation_report()` helper makes that absence machine-readable rather than silent: it always returns a report whose `verdict` is `not-measurable` with an empty `metrics` list, records `truncated` so cut-off summaries can be excluded, and names in `needs` the reference summaries, scorer and faithfulness check that would be required — and it stays `not-measurable` even when references are passed, because one reference is not a dispersion and no scorer is shipped. The upstream README's `model-index` reports ROUGE-1 42.9486, ROUGE-2 20.8149, ROUGE-L 30.6186 and ROUGE-LSUM 40.0376 on the CNN/DailyMail 3.0.0 `train` split (upstream-reported, not measured here); this repository has not reproduced them and the smoke summary is one observation, not a result.

###### Decision thresholds

`summarize` applies an implicit decision rule and names it in every result (`generation.decision_rule`): deterministic beam search with `num_beams` 4 keeps the four highest-scoring partial summaries at each step and returns the finished beam with the highest length-penalised log-probability (`length_penalty` 2.0 favours longer beams; `no_repeat_ngram_size` 3 forbids any repeated trigram; `early_stopping` true ends the search once four beams have finished). No minimum-probability cut-off exists: some token is emitted at every step, and generation stops at end-of-sequence or at `max_new_tokens` (default 141), whichever comes first, with `truncated` reporting which. `min_new_tokens` (default 55) is a floor, not a threshold: the decoder is prevented from emitting end-of-sequence before that many tokens. All four defaults are the pinned `generation_config_for_summarization.json` values (with the upstream `max_length`/`min_length` converted to new-token counts by subtracting the decoder start token); the caller may change any of them within the ceilings. No acceptance threshold was set during development and none is shipped: whether a summary is good enough to use is a judgement the deployment must make on its own references, trading a summary that omits something that matters (false negative) against one that states something the source does not (false positive) for its application.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

No metric is reported, so there is no estimation procedure or dispersion to state. Inference is deterministic given the same weights, device and library versions: dropout is disabled by `model.eval()`, `do_sample` is fixed to `False`, and no seed is required — the two smoke runs recorded in this repository produced byte-identical summaries; small numeric differences between CPU and GPU kernels can change which beam wins when two are close, which changes the wording. The pipeline emits no confidence, probability or score for the summary as a whole or for any span in it, so a caller who needs one must estimate it empirically (for example by scoring against references over a labelled set and reporting a bootstrap interval, or by an independent faithfulness classifier); the `truncated` flag is the only per-output signal and it reports a length-ceiling hit, not quality.

#### Ethical considerations and biases

###### Data

The upstream README discloses the fine-tuning data as CNN/DailyMail and the pre-training as "English language" text; the disclosure stops there — no per-article licensing, no statement of how the news corpus was obtained, and no demographic composition is given in the pinned README, and the BART pre-training corpus (books, news, web text and stories per the BART paper) is described only in the paper. CNN/DailyMail articles name real people and report on crimes, health and personal circumstances, so personal data in the training corpora is not ruled out — it is certain for the fine-tuning set — and the capacity of sequence-to-sequence models to reproduce memorised spans means it cannot be excluded from outputs either. This repository distributes code, tests and documentation; the 1.63 GB snapshot (`model.safetensors`, `config.json`, the two generation configs and tokenizer files) is git-ignored and staged locally under `weights/bart-large-cnn/` with a manifest, and no sample data or datasets are shipped — the tutorial's sample passage is synthetic prose authored in code. The operator must audit the documents they submit for personal, confidential or proprietary content and must not log or retain summaries of such documents without the same controls they would apply to the documents themselves; the pipeline performs no such check.

###### Human Life

The pipeline is not intended for decisions in health, safety, criminal justice, employment, credit, housing or any other domain central to human life, and it has not been validated or certified for any of them by anyone. Its only validation is the offline unit suite (22 tests in `tests/test_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_role_helpers.py` and `tests/test_import_boundary.py`, plus the notebook parity tests) and the CPU smoke run recorded in this repository. Where a sensitive use is foreseeable — for example summarising patient histories for a clinician, or condensing case files for a caseworker — it is admissible only with a human reading the source on every consequential outcome, an independent domain evaluation of faithfulness on representative documents, and whatever regulatory clearance the domain requires.

###### Mitigations

Implemented and inspectable in `src/bart_summarization_pipeline/pipeline.py`: (1) supply chain — `MODEL_REVISION` is a 40-hex commit; `stage_missing_files` refuses a manifest naming another model or revision and fetches only manifest-listed files at the pinned revision, and `verify_snapshot` re-hashes every file in `weights/bart-large-cnn/dimer-base-manifest.json` and raises on the first size or SHA-256 mismatch before any weight is loaded; both run before `torch` or `transformers` is imported (`tests/test_import_boundary.py`); the Hub path is taken only with `allow_download=True` and always with `revision=MODEL_REVISION`; `trust_remote_code=False` on both tokenizer and model. (2) Input integrity — `_check_text` rejects non-`str`, empty and over-`MAX_TEXT_CHARS` inputs, `_check_settings` rejects every generation setting outside its named ceiling (`MAX_NEW_TOKENS`, `MAX_NUM_BEAMS`, `LENGTH_PENALTY_RANGE`, `MAX_NO_REPEAT_NGRAM_SIZE`, and `min_new_tokens` above `max_new_tokens`), and `_check_input_tokens` rejects inputs over `MAX_INPUT_TOKENS` instead of truncating. The public `validate_inputs()` helper applies those same checks through the same private functions and returns one input manifest (schema, ceilings, per-input observations, the generation settings that will be used, verdict, findings), so a caller can record exactly what was accepted or rejected without duplicating the validation logic. (3) Reproducibility — exact `==` dependency pins, `model.eval()`, float32, `do_sample=False`, and `model_id`/`model_revision`/`device`/`source` plus the full generation settings in every result. (4) Refusals — no sampling, no fine-tuning, no chunking of over-long documents and no access to the language-model logits are exposed; a missing snapshot with `allow_download=False` raises `FileNotFoundError`. No statistical mitigation (faithfulness filtering, re-ranking against the source) is applied: the weights are redistributed unmodified.

###### Risks and harms

Hallucination: the decoder can state names, numbers, dates or causal claims that the source does not contain, in fluent prose that reads as a faithful digest; the operator and any third party described bear the harm when such a summary is relied on, and the likelihood under normal use is material — abstractive news summarizers are known to do this, and nothing here detects it. Omission: a summary of at most 141 tokens drops most of the document, and which facts survive is decided by training-corpus salience, not by the operator's needs. News register imposed: any input is rewritten as a news highlight, so a contract clause or a lab report comes back in the wrong voice with the wrong emphasis. Forced length: with the pinned `min_new_tokens` of 55, a short input yields a summary padded with copied or invented sentences. Memorised-span leakage: generation can surface phrases memorised from CNN/DailyMail or the pre-training corpus. Automation bias: a reader shown a confident summary opens the source less often. Resource exhaustion: 8 beams over 512 new tokens on a 1024-token input is the most expensive permitted call and can take minutes on a CPU host; the ceilings bound but do not eliminate this. Magnitude ranges from a misleading meeting note to a decision taken on a fabricated fact.

###### Use cases

The pipeline must not be used for surveillance, biometric or demographic profiling, or social scoring — including condensing intercepted or scraped communications about individuals into dossiers. It must not support unlawful discrimination in employment, housing, credit, insurance, education or healthcare access, for example by summarising applications so that a screener never reads the original. It must not power deceptive or manipulative applications such as presenting generated summaries as verbatim quotations or as a person's own statement, or generating misleading digests of news for distribution. Any use that violates the MIT terms of the upstream weights (the licence and copyright notice must accompany redistributed copies) or the DIMER deployment terms is prohibited. The developers identify no further prohibited use beyond these because the model's output is a short restatement of a document the operator already holds.

## Input/output contract

- `BARTSummarizationPipeline.from_pretrained(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False)`
- `summarize(text, *, max_new_tokens=141, min_new_tokens=55, num_beams=4, length_penalty=2.0, no_repeat_ngram_size=3)` — `text`: `str`, 1–40000 chars, ≤ 1024 tokens. Returns `{"summary", "generated_tokens", "input_tokens", "truncated", "stopped_by" ("eos" | "max_new_tokens"), "generation": {max_new_tokens, min_new_tokens, num_beams, length_penalty, no_repeat_ngram_size, early_stopping, do_sample, decision_rule}, "device", "source", "model_id", "model_revision"}`.
- `validate_inputs(texts, *, max_new_tokens=..., min_new_tokens=..., num_beams=..., length_penalty=..., no_repeat_ngram_size=..., names=None)` — input manifest for a batch of documents sharing one setting; raises exactly as `summarize` does.
- `evaluation_report(result, references=None, *, sample_kind="synthetic")` — always `verdict: not-measurable`; records `truncated` and names what a real evaluation needs.
- `verify_snapshot(path=None)` — returns the manifest dict with `path`; raises `FileNotFoundError` / `ValueError`.
- `stage_missing_files(path=None, *, allow_download=False, downloader=None)` — returns the relative paths fetched (`[]` if none were missing).

## Runtime

- Pins: `torch==2.14.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `tokenizers==0.22.2`, `huggingface-hub==0.36.2`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`; Python 3.12.

A first run with an explicit `min_length=0` override produced the same summary and a transformers warning; the override was removed. The CUDA path was not run.
- Tests: `pytest -q -o addopts= tests` — 22 offline tests plus the notebook parity tests, no weights required; `ruff check src tests tools` clean.
